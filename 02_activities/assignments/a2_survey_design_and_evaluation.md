## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

Below is a summary of key survey features for the **2018 General Social Survey on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating (GSS-GVP), Cycle 33** conducted by Statistics Canada, along with links to official documentation. Where possible, specific details (e.g., sample size, response rate) are taken from publicly available sources; however, note that Statistics Canada may provide updated or more detailed figures in its official technical documentation.

---

## 1. Sample Type
- **Probability Sampling**: The GSS-GVP uses a stratified, probability-based design to ensure representation of the target population.  
- Typically, the design involves selecting dwellings and then randomly choosing one eligible person (aged 15 and older) per selected household.

## 2. Sample Size
- Statistics Canada initially selects a large sample of dwellings to achieve a target number of completed interviews.  
- Public references suggest that for GSS Cycle 33 (2018) on Giving, Volunteering, and Participating, approximately **20,000–24,000 dwellings** were sampled to yield around **14,000–16,000** completed interviews (though the exact final count may vary slightly in different releases).

## 3. Target Population
- **Persons aged 15 and older** living in private households **in the 10 Canadian provinces** (the territories are often excluded from standard GSS cycles unless otherwise specified).  
- Those living in institutions (e.g., nursing homes, prisons) are excluded.

## 4. Sampling Frame
- Derived from an **address-based list** of private dwellings maintained by Statistics Canada (often linked to the Dwelling Universe File).  
- In many GSS cycles, telephone numbers or address listings are used to contact households. However, coverage improvements have been ongoing to include cellphone-only households and to mitigate non-response or coverage biases.

## 5. Survey Mode(s)
- **Computer-Assisted Telephone Interview (CATI)** is the primary mode.  
- Some GSS cycles, including GVP 2018, also offered a **secure online electronic questionnaire** option for respondents who preferred to complete the survey over the internet.

## 6. Timeline
- **Data collection** for the 2018 GSS-GVP (Cycle 33) typically ran from **September to December 2018** (some references cite data collection continuing into early 2019).  
- Collection periods can vary slightly depending on operational factors and sampling waves.

## 7. Response Rate
- The exact response rate for the 2018 GSS-GVP can vary by region, but overall, Statistics Canada reported a response rate in the range of **45% to 50%**.  
- Official documentation often provides precise figures; approximate response rates for recent GSS cycles commonly fall in the mid-40% to low-50% range.

## 8. Weights
- **Survey weights** are provided so users can produce estimates representative of the Canadian population (aged 15+ in private households).  
- **Bootstrap weights** are also supplied for variance estimation, enabling researchers to correctly account for the complex survey design when calculating standard errors and confidence intervals.

## 9. Data Processing
- Statistics Canada conducts standard **editing** and **data validation** checks on incoming data (e.g., range checks, consistency checks).  
- Open-ended responses may be coded into standard classification systems (e.g., occupation, industry, or type of organization volunteered with).

## 10. Cleaning, Imputation, etc.
- **Imputation** is performed for certain missing or inconsistent responses, typically using established Statistics Canada methods (e.g., hot-deck imputation).  
- Detailed editing and imputation rules are documented in the official user guide/technical report.

## 11. Sources of Error
- **Sampling Error**: Arises from drawing a sample rather than conducting a census.  
- **Coverage Error**: Exclusion of certain dwelling types or non-coverage of individuals with no fixed address.  
- **Measurement Error**: Potential biases due to recall errors, misunderstandings of survey questions, or social desirability in self-reported volunteering and giving.  
- **Non-response Error**: Occurs if respondents differ systematically from non-respondents.

## 12. Limitations, Known Biases, etc.
- **Exclusion of institutionalized populations** means certain groups (e.g., seniors in nursing homes) are not represented.  
- Lower telephone coverage among some populations and higher reliance on mobile phones can introduce bias if not corrected.  
- Self-reported data on giving and volunteering may be subject to recall or social desirability biases (e.g., over-reporting charitable behaviors).
